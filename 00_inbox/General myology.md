## Pennate vs Nonpennate Muscles

| a                      | Nonpennate (parallel-fibered)                          | Pennate                                                                 |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fiber arrangement**  | Parallel to tendon (= muscle line of action)           | Fibers form an angle (up to 30°) with tendon axis                       |
| **Force transmission** | Almost all force transmitted directly to tendon        | Only a portion of force transmitted (cos θ)                             |
| **At 30° pennation**   | —                                                      | Tendon force = 87% of fiber force (K' = K·cos30° = K·0.87)              |
| **Lifting height**     | Fiber shortening ≈ actual lifting height               | Lifting height > actual fiber shortening (path gain)                    |
| **Cross sections**     | Anatomic ≈ physiological cross section                 | Physiological cross section > anatomic cross section                    |
| **Force vs size**      | Greater force production (direct transmission)         | Greater force development for same size (more fibers per cross section) |
| **Key advantage**      | Efficient force transmission (no pennation angle loss) | Smaller size for similar power output                                   |

## Key Fiber Facts

- All skeletal muscle fibers ≈ same thickness (avg diameter ~60 μm)
- Fiber length varies: few mm to 20 cm
- Fiber-to-muscle length ratio: 0.2–0.6 (fiber = 20–60% of muscle length)
- Longer fibers → greater maximum shortening → greater lifting height
- Longer fibers relative to muscle → smaller physiological cross section → less lifting force
- Greater pennation angle → greater physiological cross section → greater lifting force

## Cross Section Definitions

- **Anatomic cross section** – perpendicular to the longitudinal axis, measured at the thickest part
- **Physiological cross section** – perpendicular to the muscle fibers themselves
