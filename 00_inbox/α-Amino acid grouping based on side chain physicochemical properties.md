
> This is the **functional classification** — what matters for **protein folding** and **interactions in water**.

## The four categories

|Category|Amino acids|Driving principle|
|---|---|---|
|**Nonpolar (hydrophobic)**|(Gly), Ala, Val, Leu, Ile, Met, Phe, Trp, Pro|Cluster in **protein interior** (away from water)|
|**Polar uncharged**|Ser, Thr, (Cys), Asn, Gln, Tyr, (His)|H-bond with water; found on surface or active sites|
|**Polar positive (+)** at pH 7.4|Lys⁺, Arg⁺, (His⁺)|Salt bridges, electrostatic interactions|
|**Polar negative (−)** at pH 7.4|Asp⁻, Glu⁻|Salt bridges, electrostatic interactions|

> **Parentheses = borderline cases:**
> 
> - **Gly** — technically nonpolar, but so small it behaves differently
> - **Cys** — thiol is weakly polar; also forms disulfide bonds (covalent)
> - **His** — fluctuates between charged (+1) and uncharged (0) near physiological pH

### Why this matters for folding

- **Hydrophobic effect** = dominant force driving protein folding
- Nonpolar residues → **buried in the core** (away from water)
- Polar/charged residues → **exposed on the surface** (interacting with water)
- Expanded in **Lecture 2**

## Posttranslational modifications (PTMs)

> After translation, residues can be chemically modified → new properties not encoded by DNA.

|PTM|Target|Product|Biological role|
|---|---|---|---|
|**γ-Carboxylation**|Glu → Gla|γ-carboxyglutamic acid|Ca²⁺ binding (blood coagulation factors)|
|**Hydroxylation**|Pro → 4-Hyp; Lys → 5-Hyl|Hydroxyproline, Hydroxylysine|Collagen stability (Lecture 3)|
|**Phosphorylation**|Ser, Thr, Tyr|Phospho-Ser / -Thr / -Tyr|Signal transduction switches|
|**Disulfide bond**|2 × Cys (oxidation)|Cys-S-S-Cys|Stabilizes extracellular protein 3D structure|
