## Typical Cervical Vertebrae (C3–C7)

![Pasted image 20260402124248.png](00_Assets/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260402124248.png)


|Feature|Description|
|---|---|
|**Body**|Small, square-shaped (superior view), saddle-shaped superior/inferior surfaces|
|**Vertebral foramen**|Large, triangular|
|**Uncinate processes**|Lateral projections on superior surface of body; appear ~age 10|
|**Transverse process**|Anterior bar + posterior bar → anterior & posterior tubercles|
|**Transverse foramen**|Enclosed by the bars; vertebral artery ascends C6→C1|
|**Spinal nerve sulcus**|Broad deep notch on superior surface of transverse process (C1–C3); houses emerging spinal nerve|
|**Articular processes**|Broad, flat; articular surfaces inclined ~45° from horizontal|
|**Spinous process (C3–C6)**|Short and bifid|
|**Spinous process (C7)**|Longer, thicker, not bifid; first palpable through skin = **vertebra prominens**|

## Atlas (C1) & Axis (C2)

![Pasted image 20260402124700.png](00_Assets/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260402124700.png)

|Feature|Atlas (C1)|Axis (C2)|
|---|---|---|
|**Key distinction**|No body, no spinous process|Has the **dens (odontoid process)** projecting superiorly|
|**Shape**|Ring-shaped: anterior arch + posterior arch|Has a body (fused with atlas's "missing" body = the dens)|
|**Superior articular facets**|Large, concave; articulate with occipital condyles (atlanto-occipital joint → nodding)|—|
|**Inferior articular facets**|Flat, circular; articulate with axis|—|
|**Movement**|Flexion/extension (yes/no nodding)|Rotation (no/no head turning) via pivot joint with atlas|
|**Transverse ligament**|Holds the dens against the anterior arch|—|

## Thoracic Vertebrae (T1–T12)

|Feature|Description|
|---|---|
|**Body**|Gradually taller and broader from superior to inferior; lower bodies become transverse oval (like lumbar)|
|**Vertebral foramen**|Roughly circular; smaller than cervical and lumbar|
|**End plates**|Rounded and triangular|
|**Spinous processes**|Long, angled sharply inferiorly; overlap each other|
|**Articular processes**|Inferior facets face anteriorly; superior facets face posteriorly → form zygapophyseal (facet) joints|
|**Transverse processes**|Angled backward; bear articular facets for ribs (except T11 & T12)|

## Costal Facets & Rib Articulation

|Vertebrae|Costal facets on body|Rib articulation pattern|
|---|---|---|
|**T2–T9**|Two on each side (superior + inferior costal facet)|Each rib articulates with its own numbered vertebra + the vertebra above|
|**T1**|—|Rib 1 articulates only with T1 body|
|**T10**|Superior costal facet only|Rib 10 articulates only with T10|
|**T11–T12**|Single facet each|Ribs 11–12 articulate only with their own vertebra|
|**Transverse process facets**|T1–T10 have them; T11–T12 do not|—|

## Lumbar Vertebrae (L1–L5)

|Feature|Description|
|---|---|
|**Body**|Large, transverse oval shape (superior view)|
|**Vertebral foramen**|Almost triangular|
|**Vertebral arches**|Massive|
|**Spinous process**|Thick, flattened on each side|
|**"Transverse processes" (costal processes)**|Phylogenetically = rudimentary ribs; not homologous with true transverse processes of other vertebrae|
|**Accessory process**|Small, pointed eminence at the root of each costal process (= the actual transverse process)|
|**Superior articular processes**|Massive; facets slightly concave, face **medially**; nearly vertical/sagittal orientation|
|**Inferior articular processes**|Massive; facets slightly convex, face **laterally**|
|**Mammillary processes**|On lateral surfaces of superior articular processes; serve as origin/insertion for intrinsic back muscles|

## Sacrum & Coccyx

### Composition

**Sacrum:** 5 sacral vertebrae, separate at birth, fuse postnatally into a single bone. Flattened anteroposteriorly, triangular from the front. Base (superior) articulates with L5, apex (inferior) articulates with coccyx.

**Coccyx:** 3–4 rudimentary vertebrae. Only the first has some typical vertebral features.
### Landmarks

|Landmark|What it is|
|---|---|
|**Base**|Superior end; articulates with L5|
|**Apex**|Inferior end; articulates with coccyx|
|**Pelvic (anterior) surface**|Concave in sagittal and transverse planes|
|**Anterior sacral foramina**|Between the transverse ridges|
|**Transverse ridges (lines)**|4 ridges marking fusion sites of the 5 vertebrae|
|**Median sacral crest**|Midline; fused spinous processes|
|**Medial sacral crests**|Flanking midline; fused articular processes|
|**Lateral sacral crests**|Most lateral; fused transverse processes|
|**Sacral cornua**|Inferior end of medial crests; rudimentary articular processes of S5|
|**Sacral hiatus**|Opening between sacral cornua; incomplete arch of S5; access to sacral canal|
|**Lateral parts (wings)**|Fused transverse processes + rudimentary ribs|
|**Auricular surface**|Ear-shaped; on lateral part; articulates with ilium|
|**Superior articular processes**|At the top; face posteriorly|
|**Coccygeal cornua**|Superior articular processes of coccyx|

### Sacrococcygeal Joint

| Type                      | Cartilaginous joint (symphysis); connected by a cartilaginous disk                    |
|:------------------------- |:------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Movement**              | Passive forward and backward motion                                                   |
| **Clinical significance** | Backward motion increases anteroposterior diameter of pelvic outlet during childbirth |

## Ligaments of the Spinal Column

### Vertebral Body Ligaments

|Ligament|Description|
|---|---|
|Anterior longitudinal ligament|Runs broadly on anterior surface of vertebral bodies, skull base → sacrum. Deep fibers bind adjacent vertebrae, superficial fibers span multiple segments. Firmly attached to bodies, loosely to disks|
|Posterior longitudinal ligament|Posterior surface of vertebral bodies, clivus → sacral canal. Narrow over bodies, broadens at disk levels. Thinner than anterior. Lateral gaps → predisposes to lateral disk herniation|

### Vertebral Arch Ligaments

| Ligament                        | Description                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ligamenta flava                 | Connect laminae of adjacent vertebral arches. Mainly elastic fibers (yellow color). Under tension when erect → stabilize spine in sagittal plane, limit forward flexion |
| Interspinous ligaments          | Between adjacent spinous processes                                                                                                                                      |
| Supraspinous ligament           | Along the tips of spinous processes                                                                                                                                     |
| Nuchal ligament (cervical only) | Broadened sagittal extension of supraspinous ligament; external occipital protuberance → C7 (vertebra prominens)                                                        |
| Intertransverse ligaments       | Between tips of transverse processes; limit rocking movements between vertebrae                                                                                         |

## Muscles Acting on the Vertebral Column

> **Erector spinae = iliocostalis + longissimus + spinalis.** Collectively known as the sacrospinal system. Splenius belongs to the spinotransverse system and is not part of the erector spinae.

### Lateral Tract

|Muscle|Origin|Insertion|Innervation|Action|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**Iliocostalis lumborum**|Sacrum, iliac crest, thoracolumbar fascia|Angles of ribs 6–12|Posterior rami of spinal nerves|Bilateral: extension. Unilateral: lateral flexion|
|**Iliocostalis thoracis**|Angles of ribs 7–12|Angles of ribs 1–6|Posterior rami|Same|
|**Iliocostalis cervicis**|Angles of ribs 3–6|Transverse processes C4–C6|Posterior rami|Same|
|**Longissimus thoracis**|Sacrum, spinous processes of lumbar/lower thoracic|Transverse processes of thoracic vertebrae, between tubercle and angle of ribs|Posterior rami|Bilateral: extension. Unilateral: lateral flexion|
|**Longissimus cervicis**|Transverse processes T1–T6|Transverse processes C2–C5|Posterior rami|Same|
|**Splenius cervicis**|Spinous processes T3–T6|Transverse processes C1–C3|Posterior rami|Bilateral: extension. Unilateral: ipsilateral rotation, lateral flexion|
|**Intertransversarii**|Transverse process|Adjacent transverse process|Posterior (and anterior) rami|Lateral flexion, segmental stabilization|
|**Levatores costarum** (breves / longi)|Transverse processes C7–T11|Rib below (breves: next rib; longi: skip one)|Posterior rami|Elevate ribs, lateral flexion|
![Pasted image 20260402132210.png](00_Assets/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260402132210.png)

### Medial Tract

|Muscle|Origin|Insertion|Innervation|Action|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**Interspinales**|Spinous process|Adjacent spinous process|Posterior rami|Extension, segmental stabilization|
|**Spinalis thoracis**|Spinous processes T11–L2|Spinous processes T2–T8|Posterior rami|Extension|
|**Spinalis cervicis**|Spinous processes T1–T2|Spinous process of axis (C2)|Posterior rami|Extension|
|**Rotatores breves**|Transverse process|Lamina/spinous process of vertebra 1 above|Posterior rami|Contralateral rotation, extension, stabilization|
|**Rotatores longi**|Transverse process|Lamina/spinous process of vertebra 2 above|Posterior rami|Same|
|**Multifidus**|Sacrum, iliac crest, transverse processes|Spinous processes 2–4 segments above|Posterior rami|Contralateral rotation, extension, stabilization|
|**Semispinalis thoracis**|Transverse processes T6–T12|Spinous processes C6–T4|Posterior rami|Bilateral: extension. Unilateral: contralateral rotation|
|**Semispinalis cervicis**|Transverse processes T1–T6|Spinous processes C2–C5|Posterior rami|Same|

### Key Pattern Summary

|a|Lateral tract|Medial tract|
|---|---|---|
|**General direction**|Inferolateral → superolateral|Transverse process → spinous process (ascending)|
|**Innervation**|All posterior rami|All posterior rami|
|**Bilateral action**|Extension|Extension|
|**Unilateral action**|**Ipsilateral** lateral flexion|**Contralateral** rotation (except interspinales/spinalis)|
|**Blood supply**|Segmental: posterior intercostal aa., lumbar aa., deep cervical a.|Same|
