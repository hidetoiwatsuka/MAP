
## 1.1 Why proteins matter (context)

- Proteins = ~50% of human body dry weight
- ~20,000 human genes encode different proteins
- Built from **20 proteinogenic amino acids** (encoded by DNA codons)

|Role|Examples|
|---|---|
|Structural|Cytoskeleton, extracellular matrix|
|Movement|Actin, myosin (contractile)|
|Catalysis|Enzymes|
|Transport|Channels, carriers (membrane / cytosolic / extracellular)|
|Signaling|Receptors, hormones|

### Protein classification

|Criterion|Categories|
|---|---|
|Solubility|Soluble vs. membrane|
|Shape|Globular (axial ratio < 3) vs. fibrous|
|Composition|Simple vs. conjugated (+ lipid, carbohydrate, metal, etc.)|

## 1.2 General structure of an α-amino acid

```
        H
        |
   H₂N—Cα—COOH
        |
        R   ← side chain (20 versions)
```

|Component|Name|Role|
|---|---|---|
|**−NH₂**|α-amino group|Common to all; becomes −NH₃⁺ at low pH|
|**−COOH**|α-carboxyl group|Common to all; becomes −COO⁻ at high pH|
|**Cα**|α-carbon|Central tetrahedral carbon|
|**R**|Side chain|Defines the identity of each amino acid|

- The "α" means these groups are attached to the **same carbon** (the α-carbon)

## 1.3 Stereochemistry

> **Key fact:** All natural proteinogenic α-amino acids are **L-isomers**.

|Concept|Detail|
|---|---|
|**Chirality**|Cα has 4 different substituents → chiral center → 2 mirror-image forms (enantiomers)|
|**Exception**|Glycine (R = H) → only 2 different substituents → **achiral**|
|**L vs. D**|Fischer projection convention; biology uses exclusively **L-amino acids**|
|**Why only L?**|Evolutionary selection — ribosomes only incorporate L-forms|

- Extra chiral centers exist in **Isoleucine** (2 chiral centers) and **Threonine** (2 chiral centers)
