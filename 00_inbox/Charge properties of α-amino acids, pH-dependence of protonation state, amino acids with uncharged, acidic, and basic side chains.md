
## 2.1 The zwitterion concept

> At physiological pH (~7.4), amino acids carry **both** a positive and a negative charge simultaneously.

**How it works — pH-dependent protonation:**

|Group|Low pH (acidic)|Physiological pH (~7.4)|High pH (basic)|
|---|---|---|---|
|α-amino|−NH₃⁺ (protonated)|−NH₃⁺ (still protonated)|−NH₂ (deprotonated)|
|α-carboxyl|−COOH (protonated)|−COO⁻ (deprotonated)|−COO⁻ (deprotonated)|
|**Net charge**|**+1**|**0 (zwitterion)**|**−1**|

### pKa values of backbone groups

|Group|pKa|Meaning|
|---|---|---|
|α-carboxyl|**~2**|Loses proton at very low pH → negative|
|α-amino|**~9–10**|Holds proton until high pH → positive|

## 2.2 Ionizable side chains

> Some R groups carry **additional ionizable groups** that shift the net charge depending on pH.

### Acidic side chains (proton donors → negative at pH 7.4)

|Residue|Group|pKa-R|State at pH 7.4|Charge|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**Asp (D)**|β-carboxyl|~4|Deprotonated (−COO⁻)|**−1**|
|**Glu (E)**|γ-carboxyl|~4|Deprotonated (−COO⁻)|**−1**|

### Basic side chains (proton acceptors → positive at pH 7.4)

|Residue|Group|pKa-R|State at pH 7.4|Charge|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**Lys (K)**|ε-amino|~10.5|Protonated (−NH₃⁺)|**+1**|
|**Arg (R)**|Guanidino|~12.5|Protonated|**+1** (essentially always)|
|**His (H)**|Imidazole|**~6**|**~50% protonated**|**0 to +1**|

### Other ionizable side chains (uncharged at pH 7.4)

|Residue|Group|pKa-R|State at pH 7.4|Charge|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**Cys (C)**|Thiol (−SH)|~8.3|Mostly protonated|**0**|
|**Tyr (Y)**|Phenol (−OH)|~10|Protonated|**0**|

---

## 2.3 The His wildcard

> **Exam-critical:** Histidine's pKa (~6) is the closest to physiological pH of any amino acid.

- At pH 7.4 → partially protonated → can act as **both proton donor AND acceptor**
- This makes His uniquely valuable in **enzyme active sites** (acid-base catalysis)
- Small pH shifts around 7 can flip His between charged and uncharged states
