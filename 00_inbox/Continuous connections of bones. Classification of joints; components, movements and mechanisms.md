## Different Types of Joints

### False joints (Synarthroses)

Continuously connected by connective tissue, cartilage, or bone; low to moderate degree of mobility.

- **Syndesmoses** – fibrous joints, e.g., newborn fontanelles
- **Synchondroses** – cartilaginous joints, e.g., spinal disks; if the intervening tissue is mostly fibrocartilaginous, the joint is called a **symphysis**, e.g., pubic symphysis
- **Synostoses** – sites of bony fusion, e.g., sacral bone

### True joints

Separated by a joint space; mobility is variable, depending on the attached ligaments (= discontinuous connection).

- **Diarthroses** – joints with varying degrees of freedom; classified according to:
    - Shape and arrangement of articular surfaces (spheroidal, ellipsoidal, and saddle joints)
    - Number of joint axes
    - Number of degrees of freedom
- **Amphiarthroses** – stiff joints, e.g., iliosacral joint

## Correlation Between Form and Movement Capabilities of a Joint

Joint movements are determined by the geometry of the articulating surfaces. Two basic movement types:

- **Translational movement**
- **Rotational movement**

Translational movements are usually limited to 1–2 axes of motion. Rotational movements (in spheroidal joints) can have up to 3 axes.

## Convex-Concave Rule

Describes the relationship between sliding motion and angular rotation:

- **Convex partner** moves in angular direction → rotational motion inside the joint is in the **opposite** direction
- **Concave partner** moves in angular direction → rotational motion inside the joint is in the **same** direction

### movement

|Movement pair|Description|
|---|---|
|**Flexion / Extension**|Decreasing / increasing the angle between two bones|
|**Hyperextension**|Extension beyond anatomical position|
|**Abduction / Adduction**|Moving away from / toward the midline|
|**Medial rotation / Lateral rotation**|Rotating toward / away from the midline|
|**Pronation / Supination**|Forearm: palm faces down (posterior) / up (anterior)|
|**Dorsiflexion / Plantarflexion**|Foot: pulling upward / pointing downward|
|**Inversion / Eversion**|Foot: sole turns medially / laterally|
|**Opposition / Reposition**|Thumb touches fingertips / returns to anatomical position|
|**Protraction / Retraction**|Scapula moves anteriorly / posteriorly|
|**Elevation / Depression**|Raising / lowering (e.g., shrugging)|
|**Circumduction**|Cone-shaped circular movement combining flexion, extension, abduction, adduction|
