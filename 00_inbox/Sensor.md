
## The Big Picture

The nervous system has to answer two questions about any stimulus:

1. **Is something happening?** → Receptor potential
2. **How intense is it?** → Psychophysical laws (Weber-Fechner, Stevens)

## 1. Receptor Potential

The receptor cell converts a physical stimulus (light, pressure, sound, etc.) into a change in membrane voltage.

|Property|Detail|
|---|---|
|Type|**Graded potential** (not action potential)|
|Amplitude|Scales with stimulus intensity — stronger stimulus = bigger potential|
|Threshold|**None** — any stimulus produces some response|
|Duration|Varies with stimulus duration|

> The receptor potential does **not** travel to the brain. It triggers action potentials in the afferent neuron, and **frequency** of those APs encodes intensity.

---

## 2. Weber-Fechner Law

**Formula:** `Ψ = k · log(φ/φ₀)`

- **Ψ** = magnitude of sensation (what you perceive)
- **φ** = stimulus intensity
- **φ₀** = reference / threshold intensity
- **k** = constant

**In plain English:**  
Doubling the stimulus does **not** double the sensation. Each equal _ratio_ increase in stimulus produces an equal _step_ increase in sensation. It's a **logarithmic** relationship.

**Example:** Going from 1 candle to 2 feels like the same brightness jump as going from 100 candles to 200 — because the _ratio_ is the same (×2).

---

## 3. Stevens' Power Law

**Formula:** `Ψ = k · (φ/φ₀)ⁿ`

- Same variables as above
- **n** = exponent that varies by modality

**In plain English:**  
Stevens improved on Weber-Fechner by using a **power function** instead of a log. The exponent _n_ changes depending on what sense you're using.

|Modality|n|Meaning|
|---|---|---|
|Brightness|< 1|Sensation grows slowly|
|Pain|> 1|Sensation grows fast|
|Length|≈ 1|Nearly linear|

Stevens' law is **more accurate** and applies to **all sensory modalities**.

---

## 4. Side-by-Side Comparison

||Weber-Fechner|Stevens|
|---|---|---|
|**Formula**|`Ψ = k · log(φ/φ₀)`|`Ψ = k · (φ/φ₀)ⁿ`|
|**Function type**|Logarithmic|Power function|
|**Scope**|General|General (more accurate)|
|**Key insight**|Sensation ∝ log of relative stimulus|Sensation ∝ power of relative stimulus|

---

## 5. What Is the Physical Stimulus (φ)?

The "physical stimulus" means the actual measurable physical quantity that activates the receptor.

**Example — Heaviness sensation:**

- φ = **Weight** (force in Newtons), not mass (kg)
- Gravity acts on mass to produce weight → that force activates mechanoreceptors

---

## Memory Hooks

- **Receptor potential** = graded, no threshold, scales with intensity
- **Weber-Fechner** = **log** (old law, simpler)
- **Stevens** = **power** (newer, better, has exponent _n_)
- Both laws describe **stimulus → sensation**, not stimulus → receptor potential
